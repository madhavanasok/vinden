# Vinden roles and UX flows

Oct 21, 2026 · @Oli & Hue

Vinden has 18 roles in three groups: people with Vinden accounts, students with Vinden Club roles, and people outside the platform. Eight core flows connect them, and nearly every flow either creates Proof of Work, checks it, or uses it to make a decision.

## At a glance

&#91;embedded content: Vinden ecosystem · 7 actors around Proof of Work\]

Every role touches Proof of Work: students make it, AI and the Core Team check it, alumni and professionals vouch for it, and companies set the briefs behind it and hire from it. Money moves one way: companies pay the club for each brief, and Vinden takes no share.

## Flow 1: Sign up and onboarding

Onboarding ends with a first piece of work, because an empty profile has nothing to verify or vouch for. Who: students, early-career professionals, founders and freelancers. Companies sign up separately on the website (Flow 7).

1. Sign up with an email address.
2. Pick what describes you: student, early-career professional, founder or freelancer.
3. Students add their college. If it has a Vinden Club, they see it and can apply (Flow 5).
4. Pick skills and interests, which shape Home and what Vinden recommends.
5. Add past roles now or later, and verify them (Flow 4).
6. Post a first piece of work (Flow 2). Profile strength shows what is still missing.

## Flow 2: Post and verify Proof of Work

&#91;embedded content: Posting Proof of Work · 7 steps, 2 return loops\]

Any failed check sends the work back to its author, and resubmitted work goes through every check again. Club review applies only to club work, and company sign-off only to paid briefs, where the company also decides whether the work is public or private. Solo work skips the teammate step.

## Flow 3: Vouch for work

Anyone with a Vinden account can vouch, and a vouch counts for more when the voucher has a stronger profile, so it means more than a like.

1. Open a piece of work and choose Vouch for this work.
2. Pick the skills you are vouching for, and add a note if you want to.
3. Vinden shows your vouch weight, set by your profile and experience, before you confirm.
4. The vouch appears on the work, the score goes up and the author is notified.
5. You can undo your vouch later.

**Asking for a vouch.** An author can ask specific people to vouch, for example a mentor who saw the work. They get a link, sign in, and land on step 1.

**Rules to settle:** whether teammates can vouch for their own team's work (the demo blocks it), and limits that stop members of one club from trading vouches.

## Flow 4: Verify experience

&#91;embedded content: Experience verification · 2 paths\]

The offer letter path exists for people job-hunting quietly: only Vinden's reviewers see the letter, and the profile shows the badge, never the letter. If a manager doesn't reply after a reminder, the person can switch to the offer letter; if the manager declines, the role stays unverified. Students who interned can usually get a quick manager confirmation.

## Flow 5: Join a Vinden Club

Any student at a college with a chapter can apply. Selection is on motivation and ownership, not marks, and there is never a fee. The Core Team runs every step.

1. **Motivation form.** The student applies in the app.
2. **Screening.** The Core Team shortlists applicants.
3. **Interview.**
4. **Practical task.** A short piece of work, which can become the applicant's first Proof of Work.
5. **Final selection.** About 30 members per club, who then start the training programme (Flow 6).

Each step can end in a decline, and the applicant should hear back at every stage. In the app, the Core Team needs an applications board to move people through these stages and record why.

## Flow 6: The club learning loop

&#91;embedded content: Club learning loop · 6 steps\]

The docs call this the core learning loop: learn, work, create Proof of Work, get it verified, build a portfolio, access opportunities. Proof of Work is mandatory for eligible club work, so posting has to be how a task gets marked done, not an extra step. In the app, Learning & Development need a way to publish tasks with due dates, and the Quality team need a review queue.

## Flow 7: A paid problem statement, end to end

&#91;embedded content: Paid problem statement · 4 lanes, 14 steps\]

After sign-off, two things happen at once: the company pays the club, and the students post the work as Proof of Work (Flow 2). Vinden takes no share of the fee. A company that came for one problem stays in the network and can find talent through the work itself. The docs don't yet say how a brief's team is chosen or how payment is handled; see the gaps below.

## Flow 8: A recruiter finds and contacts talent

Recruiters build a shortlist from verified work instead of keyword filters, which is the hiring problem raised in the meeting. Who: recruiters and hiring managers on company accounts.

1. Search by skill, or browse by category, college or club.
2. Filter by vouch weight and by the checks the work has passed.
3. Scan the cards: the work, its score and its checks.
4. Open a piece of Proof of Work to see who did what and who vouched.
5. Open the student's profile: all their verified work, their experience and their journey.
6. Save them to a shortlist, then message them or invite them to an opening.

Contacting students is the likeliest place for Vinden to earn money, since clubs keep every brief fee and students pay nothing. The docs don't say.

## Gaps the docs leave open

Each of these changes at least one flow above, so each needs a decision from the client before the flows are final.

| Gap | Flows it affects | What needs deciding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How trust signals combine | 2, 3, 4, 8 | AI checks, club review, vouches and experience checks are each described, but not how they add up to what a recruiter sees |
| Who reviews the reviewers | 2 | Core Team members' own work is verified by their own club's Quality team; central or cross-club review would keep standards even |
| Confidential company work | 2, 7 | Proof of Work is mandatory and public by default, but a brief can involve private company data |
| How a brief's team is chosen | 7 | The docs say the club "organises a student team", not whether members apply, are picked, or both |
| Payments | 7 | Whether companies pay through Vinden or directly, and whether a student club can legally receive and hold the money |
| How Vinden earns | 8 | Clubs keep every brief fee and students pay nothing, so the company side is the likely source |
| Is the club separate from hiring? | 6, 7, 8 | The meeting calls the club separate from hiring; the operating model's flywheel runs on companies finding club talent |
| Core Team size | 5 | Learning & Development is 4 people in the role list and 3 in the summary, making the Core Team 13 or 12 |
| Manager doesn't reply | 4 | What happens when a manager has left the company or never answers the email |
