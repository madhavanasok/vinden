# Vinden roles and UX flows

Version 2 · Oct 30, 2026 · @Oli & Hue

Vinden's roles fall into six groups: people who can use it without signing in, account holders, Vinden Club roles, community roles, companies and the Vinden team. Ten core flows connect them, and nearly every flow either creates Proof of Work, checks it, or uses it to make a decision.

The client-facing copy of this document is the Notion page "Vinden roles and flows (v2, for review)". Version 1 (Oct 21) is the Claude Doc https://claude.ai/artifact/WoBavP2nou7RzyXo1iHTwj.

## At a glance

Proof of Work sits in the middle. Students make it, clubs train people and check it, alumni and professionals vouch for it, and companies, recruiters and founders use it to find talent. Communities carry updates and events, and each club's community is how students hear about its next recruitment. Money moves one way: companies pay the club for each paid project, and Vinden takes no share.

The loop: Learn → Work → Post Proof of Work → Checked → Vouched → Found.

## Roles

One person can hold several roles at once: a student can follow communities, be a club member and sit on the Core Team. The From column says where each role comes from; "Our proposal" marks a split the client hasn't confirmed.

**People who can use Vinden without signing in**

| Role | Who they are | What they can do | From |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiter or HR | Hiring staff and the point of contact for interviews | View and search public Proof of Work, profiles and club portfolios | Review call |
| Hiring manager | A candidate's current or former manager | Confirm a past role from an emailed link; view the candidate's work | Meeting, review call |
| Founder | Especially early-stage founders, who judge candidates themselves | View work and profiles; bring a problem to a club through Outreach | Review call |

**Vinden Club roles**

A Vinden Club is selective: about 30 members a year, usually second-year students, led by a Core Team of 10–12. Most clubs belong to one college, but a club can also be an independent body serving several colleges. The club's seniors (third-year students) design the courses.

## Flow 1: Sign up and onboarding (changed)

Get people into the app in about a minute and collect the rest of the profile later. Who: students, early-career professionals, founders and freelancers. Companies sign up separately on the website (Flow 7). Harsh asked for this flow to be reworked; it is up for review at the follow-up call.

1. Sign up with an email address.
2. Pick what describes you: student, early-career professional, founder or freelancer. This is the only step that can't be skipped.
3. About you (skippable). Students add their college and year of study; others add their current role and organisation.
4. Communities (skippable). Suggestions come from college and interests. If the college has a Vinden Club, its community comes first: "Follow for updates, events and the next recruitment."
5. Land on Home. The Your journey card shows profile strength and the next thing to add: skills and interests, past roles (Flow 4), a résumé, then a first Proof of Work (Flow 2).

Sign-up never offers to join a Vinden Club; students hear about recruitment through the club's community and apply in Flow 5. Anything skipped appears in profile strength. Changed from version 1: onboarding no longer ends with posting work.

**To decide:** whether college and year are required for students (we suggest skippable, required only when applying to a club), and whether founder and freelancer are separate choices (we suggest yes, with the same features).

## Flow 2: Post and verify Proof of Work

1. Describe the work: title, kind (club task, paid brief, internship, independent project), category, what you did, files and links.
2. Solo or team. For a team, add teammates with their roles and shares, which add up to 100%.
3. Teammates confirm their shares.
4. AI check of the files and claims, which takes minutes.
5. Club review by Proof of Work & Quality. Club work only.
6. Company sign-off. Paid briefs only; the company also decides whether the work is public or private.
7. Published: the checks appear on the work, and vouching opens.

Any failed check sends the work back to its author with a reason, and resubmitted work goes through every check again. Solo work skips the teammate steps. If the author adds no images, Vinden generates a cover from the kind of work, its title and its file type, in a tone assigned from the work, so every piece of work has a proper card.

## Flow 3: Vouch for work

Anyone with a Vinden account can vouch, and a vouch counts for more when the voucher has a stronger profile, so it means more than a like.

1. Open a piece of work and choose Vouch for this work.
2. Pick the skills you are vouching for, and add a note if you want to.
3. Vinden shows your vouch weight, set by your profile and experience, before you confirm.
4. The vouch appears on the work, the score goes up and the author is notified.
5. You can undo your vouch later.

**Asking for a vouch.** An author can ask specific people to vouch, for example a mentor who saw the work. They get a link, sign in, and land on step 1.

**Rules to settle:** limits that stop members of one club from trading vouches. Teammates can't vouch for their own team's work (the demo blocks it).

## Flow 4: Verify experience

Two paths:
- **Manager email:** the manager clicks a link, with no account needed. The profile shows "Manager confirmed".
- **Private offer letter:** only Vinden's reviewers see the letter. The profile shows "Offer letter verified · private", never the letter.

The offer letter path exists for people job-hunting quietly. If a manager doesn't reply after a reminder, the person can switch to the offer letter; if the manager declines, the role stays unverified. Students who interned can usually get a quick manager confirmation.

## Flow 5: Vinden Club: starting a club and yearly recruitment (changed)

The club is exclusive, so joining it is a yearly selection, not an onboarding step. Selection is on motivation and ownership, not marks, and there is never a fee.

**Starting a club**
1. A founding Core Team applies to the Vinden team to start a club: for one college, a group of colleges, or as an independent club.
2. The Vinden team approves it. The club page and the club's community (Flow 9) open straight away.

**Yearly recruitment**
1. Community & Events announce recruitment in the club's community, and every follower is notified.
2. Eligible students apply with a motivation form while recruitment is open.
3. Screening by the Core Team.
4. Interview.
5. Practical task, which can become the applicant's first Proof of Work.
6. Final selection of about 30 members, usually second-year students.
7. New members get the interest survey (Flow 6).

Each step can end in a decline, and the applicant hears back at every stage. Applicants who aren't selected keep following the community and can apply again next year. In the app, the Core Team needs an applications board to move people through these stages and record why.

**To decide:** which years can apply (we suggest each club sets it, second year by default); how an applicant's college is confirmed when a club serves several colleges (we suggest a college email at application); who oversees independent clubs (we suggest the Vinden team approves and audits them like any club).

## Flow 6: Learning and development (changed)

The docs call this the core learning loop: learn, work, create Proof of Work, get it verified, build a portfolio, access opportunities. The review call added how the learning is planned.

1. After recruitment, Learning & Development send every new member an interest survey.
2. The top interests set the course plan: which courses to run and how many cohorts. Each club follows its college's focus, for example AI and design rather than web development.
3. The seniors design the courses: lessons, tasks, projects and resources, plus alumni and industry sessions.
4. Members join cohorts by interest. Not every member takes every course.
5. Each course ends in tasks and projects. Posting Proof of Work is how a task gets marked done, not an extra step (Flow 2).
6. Proof of Work & Quality review the work through a review queue. Progress shows on the club page and each member's journey.

**To decide:** whether a central library of courses exists that clubs can pick from (Madhavan asked; Harsh answered with the seniors-design model only). We suggest club-made courses plus Vinden templates at launch, a shared library later.

## Flow 7: A paid problem statement, end to end

1. The company admin registers on the website (the same account works in the app) and submits a problem with scope, timeline and fee. Outreach can submit one on a company's behalf.
2. The Core Team accepts the problem statement.
3. The club forms a team (open question below).
4. The team works, and Proof of Work & Quality review the work before it is delivered.
5. The company signs off and decides whether the work is public or private.
6. Two things happen at once: the company pays the club, and the team posts the work through Flow 2.
7. The work joins the club's portfolio (Flow 10).

Vinden takes no share of the fee. A company that came for one problem stays in the network and can find talent through the work itself.

**To decide:** who manages, schedules and staffs a paid project inside the club. We suggest an Outreach member leads it, the President approves, members apply to join, and the Core Team picks the team.

## Flow 8: Finding talent, with or without signing in (changed)

Recruiters, HR, hiring managers and founders build a shortlist from verified work instead of keyword filters, which is the hiring problem raised in the first meeting. Viewing needs no account.

1. Open a shared link, or search by skill, category, college or club.
2. Filter by vouch weight and by the checks the work has passed.
3. Scan the cards: the work, its score and its checks.
4. Open a piece of Proof of Work to see who did what and who vouched.
5. Open the person's profile or the club's portfolio.
6. To save, shortlist, message or invite someone to an opening, sign in. Recruiters use a company account.

Contacting students is the likeliest place for Vinden to earn money, since clubs keep every brief fee and students pay nothing. The docs don't say.

## Flow 9: Communities (new)

Communities let anyone follow topics and clubs while the club itself stays selective.

| Type | Created by | Who can join | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform community | Vinden team | Anyone; open | Product and growth, design, data |
| Club community | Each Vinden Club, run by Community & Events | Anyone; open. Following is not membership | Vinden Club Meridian |
| Closed community | Its admin | By approval | A club's alumni circle, a founders' group |

**Joining an open community:** find it in sign-up, search, a club page or a shared link; choose Join; see its updates and events and get notified.

**Joining a closed community:** choose Request to join (the button shows Requested); the admin approves or declines; the person is notified either way.

A club's community carries announcements, events, sessions, showcases and recruitment news, posted by the Core Team. A first-year can follow from day one and gets the recruitment announcement. Every community shows as a Community card: name, type, followers, latest update, and Join, Joined, Request to join or Requested.

**To decide:** who can create communities (we suggest the Vinden team and clubs at launch, anyone later); whether followers can post (we suggest admins post, followers react and RSVP at launch); who moderates.

## Flow 10: Showcasing work at three levels (new)

| Level | Where it shows | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person | Their profile | All their verified work, with their part in team work |
| A team | The Proof of Work page, and each teammate's profile | Every teammate's role and share |
| A club | The club's portfolio | Every club task and paid brief, with totals: work verified, money earned, companies worked with |

**To decide:** how private paid work appears in a club portfolio. We suggest one line such as "Confidential project for a fintech company", with no details.
